Slide 3

Why this clinic exists

  • 59.5 vs 24.2
  • Pregnancy-related deaths per 100,000 live births in Georgia — non-Hispanic Black vs non-Hispanic white women, 2019–2021 (Georgia DPH Maternal Mortality Review Committee)
  • 85%
  • of reviewed pregnancy-related deaths had at least some chance of being prevented; 27% occurred 43–365 days postpartum (Georgia DPH)
  • 1 in 2
  • Georgia counties is a maternity care desert — no hospital or birth center with labor and delivery (March of Dimes, 2026)
  • Athens-Clarke County: 24.3% poverty (nearly double Georgia's 12.6%), 11.2% uninsured. Oglethorpe and Madison Counties next door have no hospital at all.
  • Late or absent prenatal care is the most changeable factor behind these numbers. A mobile van in California brought women into prenatal care three weeks earlier (Edgerley et al. 2007). That is the job.

Facilitator notes: Sources: Georgia DPH Maternal Mortality Report 2019–2021; March of Dimes 2026 report; U.S. Census ACS 2024; Edgerley et al., Maternal and Child Health Journal 2007.

Slide 4

Our model

  • Scheduled clinic days at trusted community sites, run by volunteers under a medical director
  • What we provide
  • Prenatal and postpartum visits
  • Blood-pressure and glucose screening
  • Nutrition counseling and prenatal vitamins
  • Perinatal depression and anxiety screening
  • Health education (warning signs, birth planning, breastfeeding, newborn care)
  • Navigation: Medicaid, WIC, delivery-hospital registration, transport, doulas
  • Telehealth follow-up between visits
  • Where
  • Athens-Clarke County: ACC Library (Baxter St), Lay Park, West Broad Farmers Market, Kroger College Station Rd, Clarke Central area, partner churches — all on Athens Transit routes.
  • Phase 2: monthly days in Oglethorpe and Madison Counties; Phase 3: Elbert County.
  • How we grow
  • Phase 1 — pop-up clinic days in partner facilities, no vehicle
  • Phase 2 — leased or donated van; county days
  • Phase 3 — owned mobile unit, paid clinical staff, Medicaid billing
  • Phase 4 — we train other free clinics to run the same service line
Slide 5

Your role

  • Every role matters; every role has limits
  • Intake and navigation volunteer — welcome, registration, insurance/WIC screening, appointment and referral follow-up
  • Clinical volunteer (licensed RN, NP, CNM, MD/DO; MA under supervision) — vitals, screening, counseling under protocols; Module 2 required
  • Health educator — warning-signs and birth-planning sessions using approved materials
  • Community health worker / doula liaison — outreach, trust-building, home-visit and doula referrals
  • Logistics and driver — site setup, supplies, equipment transport, safety
  • Data and administration — documentation, follow-up calls, outcome tracking
  • Licensed volunteers are enrolled in the Georgia Volunteer Health Care Program, which provides liability protection when serving eligible uninsured patients at a qualifying clinic.
Slide 6

Scope and referral rules

  • The safety of this clinic is its narrow scope
  • We do
  • Routine prenatal and postpartum visits under written protocols
  • Screen: blood pressure at every visit, urine dipstick, point-of-care glucose, EPDS
  • Counsel and educate
  • Link every woman to a delivery hospital and an obstetric provider of record
  • Refer the same day for any severe-range blood pressure, bleeding, severe headache, vision change, decreased fetal movement, or thoughts of self-harm
  • We never do
  • Deliveries or labor management
  • Diagnostic ultrasound
  • Emergency care — call 911 and stay with the patient
  • Prescribing beyond the medical director's standing orders
  • Anything outside your own role or license
  • Promise a diagnosis or outcome

Facilitator notes: Walk through the escalation protocol: who calls 911, who calls the medical director, who documents. Every clinic day has a named lead clinician.

Slide 7

Respectful care and cultural humility

  • Listen first. Women in this community report not being heard by clinicians; "Hear Her" starts with us.
  • Ask, don't assume. About family, faith, food, work, transport, and what care has been like before.
  • Explain everything, every time. What the reading means, what happens next, what to watch for.
  • No judgment about insurance, timing, or past choices. Late is better than never; our job is to make the next step easy.
  • Privacy is dignity. Screen in a private space; never discuss a patient where others can hear.
  • Language and literacy. Plain words, teach-back ("tell me how you'll take these"), interpreter when needed.
Slide 8

Confidentiality and HIPAA basics

  • The rule
  • Patient information is used only to care for the patient and run the clinic. You see only what your role needs. You never share it with anyone outside the care team — including family and friends of the patient — without the patient's written permission.
  • In practice
  • No photos of patients or forms. No patient details in texts, WhatsApp, or social media. Paper forms in the locked box; laptop locked when you step away. Speak quietly; use first names only in shared spaces. Return every form at end of day.
  • If something goes wrong
  • Lost form, wrong recipient, overheard conversation: tell the day lead immediately. Reporting a mistake is expected and protected; hiding one is not.
Slide 9

Urgent maternal warning signs (CDC "Hear Her")

  • During pregnancy and up to a year after. If a woman reports any of these: get help right away.
  • Severe headache that won't go away or gets worse
  • Dizziness or fainting
  • Changes in vision
  • Fever of 100.4°F or higher
  • Extreme swelling of hands or face
  • Thoughts of harming yourself or your baby
  • Trouble breathing
  • Chest pain or fast-beating heart
  • Severe nausea and throwing up
  • Severe belly pain that doesn't go away
  • Baby's movements stopping or slowing
  • Vaginal bleeding or fluid leaking
  • Swelling, redness or pain of the leg
  • Overwhelming tiredness
  • Blood pressure 160/110 or higher at any visit = same-day referral and a call to the medical director. Source: CDC Hear Her campaign.
Slide 10

Perinatal mental health: the EPDS

  • What it is
  • The Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale: 10 questions, about 5 minutes, validated in pregnancy and postpartum. We screen at every trimester and postpartum. Black pregnant women report post-traumatic stress at four times the rate of non-Black women (Seng et al. 2011); mental-health conditions are a leading cause of pregnancy-related death in Georgia.
  • What you do with the score
  • Score 10 or higher, or any positive answer to question 10 (thoughts of self-harm): tell the day lead now. Warm hand-off to a behavioral-health partner; Georgia Crisis & Access Line 1-800-715-4225; 988 for immediate danger.
  • Lower scores: offer support resources, HMHBGA virtual classes, peer groups; re-screen next visit.
  • Never leave a woman who has disclosed self-harm thoughts alone.
Slide 11

Navigation resources

  • Georgia Medicaid / Pregnancy Medicaid and 12-month postpartum coverage — gateway.ga.gov; we help complete the application on site
  • WIC (District 10 health departments) — nutrition benefits from pregnancy through age 5
  • Georgia Family Healthline 1-888-457-4769 — Healthy Mothers, Healthy Babies Coalition of Georgia; referrals, perinatal kits, virtual classes
  • Delivery-hospital registration — Piedmont Athens Regional or St. Mary's; obstetric provider of record
  • Doula and home-visiting programs; transportation assistance — through partners and Medicaid non-emergency transport
  • Free clinics for non-maternal needs — Mercy Health Center, Athens Wellness Clinic
Slide 12

A clinic day, step by step

  • Setup and safety check (lead clinician, supplies, privacy screen, emergency kit)
  • Welcome and intake (consent, history, coverage screening)
  • Vitals and screening (BP, weight, urine, glucose, fetal heart tones by licensed volunteer)
  • Visit with clinician; EPDS; counseling
  • Education and navigation (enrollments started on site)
  • Referrals booked; next visit scheduled; telehealth check-in set
  • Documentation complete before you leave; forms locked; debrief
Slide 13

Documentation and the outcomes we track

  • This clinic is built to produce evidence other clinics can use
  • Prenatal-care initiation — share of enrolled women with a first visit in the first trimester
  • Screening rates — blood pressure every visit; EPDS each trimester and postpartum; glucose screening (target ≥ 95%)
  • Referral completion within 14 days — hypertension, mental health, hospital registration
  • Enrollment — Medicaid, postpartum Medicaid, WIC completed
  • Birth outcomes vs county baseline — preterm birth, low birth weight, severe maternal morbidity
  • Patient experience — respectful-care survey after each visit
  • Evaluation advisor: Anne O. Summers, PhD, University of Georgia. Results are shared with Mobile Health Map, the Georgia Charitable Care Network, and the National Association of Free & Charitable Clinics.

Attendance and completion records

Used at every session and kept in the volunteer file.

Attendance record

Session date, time, format (video conference or in person), facilitator, materials used, and a signed roster of every attendee with join and leave times. For video sessions, a timestamped screenshot of the participant list is attached.

Statement of completion

Each volunteer signs a statement confirming the date, duration, and content of the session, completion of the knowledge check, the role they intend to fill, and their relevant qualifications. The facilitator countersigns.

Knowledge check

A short quiz at the end of every module covering scope and referral rules, urgent warning signs, EPDS escalation, and confidentiality.
